## Before you go
In Germany, you have a legal right to a Basiskonto (basic account). This is a Girokonto (current account) that anyone can open, no matter your credit score. It works for cash payments, direct debits, transfers, standing orders, and card payments. Asylsuchende (asylum seekers), Geduldete (people with tolerated stay), and homeless people are also entitled to it. People who fled Ukraine are also seen as entitled, according to consumer advice centres.

## What to bring
- The official application form (Antrag auf Abschluss eines Basiskontovertrags). Banks and Sparkassen must offer this form, also online.
- Your Reisepass (passport) or Personalausweis (ID card) to prove your identity.
- If you are an Asylsuchende(r): the amtlicher Ankunftsnachweis (official arrival certificate) is enough.
- If you are Geduldete(r): bring the Duldungsbescheid (toleration notice).
- If you are Ukrainian and have no valid passport or secure ID card: banks must accept a Ukrainian ID document, but you must also show a document from a German authority, such as Anlaufbescheinigung, Fiktionsbescheinigung, or Meldebescheinigung.

## At the appointment
Fill in the form completely and hand it in at the bank or Sparkasse. The bank must confirm, with a date, that they received your application (on a copy or duplicate). The bank must open your account within ten Geschäftstage (business days) after you hand in the application.

## Good to know
- The Basiskonto is only for private use, not for business purposes.
- The bank does not have to give you a Dispositionskredit (overdraft), but you can agree on one separately.
- The account is not free, but fees must be "angemessen" (reasonable) under § 41 ZKG. Fees should not be higher than normal private account fees.
- A German court (Bundesgerichtshof) ruled in 2020 that a fee of 8.99 euro plus extra costs at Deutsche Bank was too high. As a guide, if your Basiskonto costs more than 6.45 euro a month and is much more expensive than a comparable private account, you may be able to challenge the fee.
- If a bank refuses to open a Basiskonto for you, or if you think fees are unfair, you can contact BaFin (Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht) using their online complaint form or by post. BaFin can start a free review process.
- You can also ask a Verbraucherzentrale (consumer advice centre) for help.
