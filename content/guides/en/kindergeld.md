## What is Kindergeld?
- Kindergeld (child benefit) is a monthly payment for parents. It does not depend on your income.
- In 2026 you get **259 EUR per child per month** (source: Bundesagentur für Arbeit, retrieved 26 Sep 2026). Since 1 January 2023 the amount is the same for every child.
- Only one person gets Kindergeld for a child, usually one parent. For several children you get one total payment. The payment date depends on your Kindergeldnummer (child benefit number).
- You normally get it until your child is 18. For older children in school, training or studies, special rules apply.

## Who can get it?
- Your child is under 18, lives in your household and you care for it regularly (this also counts for stepchildren, grandchildren and foster children).
- You live in Germany, another EU country, Norway, Liechtenstein, Iceland or Switzerland.
- **Non-German parents** can get Kindergeld if one of these applies:
  - You are a citizen of an EU country, Iceland, Liechtenstein, Norway or Switzerland and live or work in Germany. If you moved to Germany in or after August 2019, from the fourth month after arrival the conditions of the Freizügigkeitsgesetz (free movement law) must be met, for example you work, look for work, or have enough money and health insurance.
  - You have a Niederlassungserlaubnis (settlement permit), an Erlaubnis zum Daueraufenthalt-EU (EU long-term residence permit), a Blaue Karte EU, an ICT-Karte or Mobiler-ICT-Karte, or an Aufenthaltserlaubnis (residence permit) that allows you to work in Germany for at least six months. Some residence permits have extra conditions.
  - You are a citizen of Algeria, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Kosovo, Morocco, Montenegro, Serbia, Tunisia or Türkiye and work in a job with social insurance (sozialversicherungspflichtig) or get unemployment or sickness benefit.
  - You are a recognised refugee or Asylberechtigte(r) (person entitled to asylum) with a final decision.
- Usually **no** Kindergeld: if you are in Germany for study or training (Aus- oder Weiterbildung), or if you only have an Aufenthaltsgestattung (permission to stay during the asylum procedure) or a Duldung (toleration), except a Beschäftigungsduldung (toleration for employment).
- The Familienkasse checks every case. It has a separate information page for refugees from Ukraine.

## Steuer-ID (tax ID)
- You need your own Steuer-ID and your child's (steuerliche Identifikationsnummer, 11 digits). For a child under 18 this is usually the only proof you need.
- After your first Anmeldung (address registration) in Germany, the Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt, Federal Central Tax Office) gives you a Steuer-ID and sends it **by letter** to your registered address. For a baby born in Germany, the Familienkasse gets the child's number automatically from the BZSt.
- No letter 3 months after your Anmeldung? Send the BZSt a copy of your ID document and your Meldebestätigung (registration certificate). Lost the letter? Ask for it again with the BZSt online form; the number never changes.

## How to apply
- **Online** at arbeitsagentur.de ("Kindergeld online beantragen"). With a **BundID** account you can send the application directly to the Familienkasse, without printing and signing.
- **On paper:** Antrag auf Kindergeld (KG1) plus one Anlage Kind for each child. In cross-border cases (for example a parent works abroad or a child lives abroad) also the Anlage Ausland (KG 51) and an Arbeitgeberbescheinigung (employer's certificate); self-employed people can send a copy of the Gewerbeanmeldung (business registration) or a tax assessment. The forms also exist in other languages. A paper application is only valid with your signature.
- For a child born abroad, add a copy of the foreign Geburtsurkunde (birth certificate) or another official document that proves the birth.
- Send **copies only, never originals**. You can also send documents online, by post or by fax.
- Applying is **always free**, and only the Familienkasse decides. Do not pay companies that offer to apply for you.

## After you apply
- Kindergeld is paid back for up to **6 months** before the month of your application, so apply early.
- No answer after 6 weeks? Call the Familienkasse (free of charge): **0800 4 555530**. From abroad: +49 911 12031010. Have your Kindergeldnummer ready if you have one.
- Tell the Familienkasse about changes quickly (new address, child moves out, you move abroad), and answer its questionnaires before the deadline.
- If you disagree with a decision, you can object (Einspruch) within one month.
- The Familienkasse also advises on the Kinderzuschlag (extra child supplement for families with a low income). In Dresden, families who get Kinderzuschlag pay no Kita fee.
