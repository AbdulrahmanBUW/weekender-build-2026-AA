## The Kinderarzt (paediatrician)
- A Kinderärztin or Kinderarzt (paediatrician) looks after your child from birth to the teenage years: U check-ups, vaccinations, illnesses and injuries, chronic conditions, advice for parents, and referrals to specialists or therapy (for example Logopädie, speech therapy).
- Look for a practice **early**, ideally during pregnancy or right after you arrive. Think about distance and transport, opening hours, how the practice is organised, and whether you trust the doctor.
- Call practices and ask if they take neue Patienten (new patients). The "Ask for me" button can make this call for you in German.
- Cannot find a practice? If you have statutory health insurance, the **Terminservicestelle** (appointment service of the statutory health insurance doctors) also helps you to find a Kinderarzt, without a referral. Call **116 117** or use eterminservice.de. You cannot choose a specific doctor there. The service has up to one week to offer you an appointment, and the appointment should be within four weeks.

## U check-ups (U1–U9) and J1
Every child has a right to 11 check-ups: U1 to U9 (including U7a) and J1. Statutory and private health insurance pay for them if they are done in the right time window. The doctor checks growth, development, sight, hearing, speech and vaccinations, and you can ask all your questions.

| Check-up | Age of your child |
|---|---|
| U1 | right after birth |
| U2 | 3–10 days |
| U3 | 4–5 weeks |
| U4 | 3–4 months |
| U5 | 6–7 months |
| U6 | 10–12 months |
| U7 | 21–24 months |
| U7a | 34–36 months |
| U8 | 46–48 months |
| U9 | 60–64 months (before school) |
| J1 | young teenagers, about 12–14 years |

- Keep to the time windows, also if your child was born early. The dates count from the real date of birth.
- Some Krankenkassen (health insurers) pay extra check-ups (U10, U11, J2). Ask your insurer before you book.
- In August 2026 the G-BA (Gemeinsamer Bundesausschuss, the body that decides on health insurance benefits) decided on a new official check-up for children aged 9 to 10 (the new U10). It can only start after further legal steps; ask your Kinderarzt. The G-BA also decided that J1 results will in future be recorded in the gelbes Heft; until now the booklet holds the U check-ups.
- Children can have dental check-ups at the dentist from the age of 6 months.

## The gelbes Heft (yellow booklet)
- You get the gelbes Heft (Kinderuntersuchungsheft, yellow child check-up booklet) shortly after birth. The doctor writes the results of every U check-up in it. It also explains what each check-up looks at.
- The results are confidential. Kita, school or authorities may only look at them with your consent. Newer booklets (since 2017) have a fold-out Teilnahmekarte (attendance card) that only shows which check-ups were done.
- In Dresden, the Kita needs a doctor's certificate or the gelbes Heft before your child's first day.

## Vaccinations
- Kinderärzte vaccinate according to the recommendations of the STIKO (Ständige Impfkommission, standing vaccination committee). In Saxony, the health office also checks vaccinations against the recommendations of the Sächsische Impfkommission (SIKO), for example at the school-entry check.
- At the U check-ups the doctor checks whether recommended vaccinations are missing and can give them later.
- Bring any vaccination records from your home country, so the doctor can see what your child already has.
- Before Kita or school you must show proof of measles protection (Masernschutz). For the Kita you also show that your child has the vaccinations recommended for its age, or declare that you do not agree to certain vaccinations.

## What to bring to the practice
- The child's elektronische Gesundheitskarte (eGK, health insurance card)
- The gelbes Heft and the Impfausweis (vaccination card)
- A list of medicines and any doctor's letters or reports
- Your questions, written down

## Outside opening hours
- For problems that cannot wait until the next day: ärztlicher Bereitschaftsdienst (on-call doctor service), phone **116 117**.
- Emergency (for example breathing problems, unconsciousness, heavy vomiting, poisoning or serious injury): call **112** or go to a hospital with a Kinderklinik (children's hospital).
