# Parent Interview Kit

**Definition:** a kit for 3–5 short interviews (10 minutes each) with international parents at the Weekender Build venue, Sat 26.09 and Sun 27.09. It replaces the board task "Interview 3–5 international parents at the venue".
**Why it matters for us:** Maria is a proto-persona ([[Idea B - Dies-Das-Ana-Nas]], [[Personas]]). Before the demo we want a few real stories that show whether the riskiest parts of "Ask for me" hold up. The result is 3 bullets for the demo and the [[Monday-Morning Plan]].
**Related:** [[Merged Concept]] · [[Pitch Kit v2 (merged)]] · [[Personas]] · [[Data minimisation - no symptoms]] · [[AI discloses itself in first sentence]] · [[Never impersonate the user]] · [[Monday-Morning Plan]]

> **Privacy rules: read before every interview**
> - Never write down names (parent, child, partner, teacher, doctor), phone numbers, emails, addresses or birthdays.
> - No health data: no symptoms, diagnoses, allergies, development or pregnancy. If a parent starts, say: *"No need to tell me why. I only want to know how the call went."* Write "health topic, not recorded".
> - Do not ask about nationality, residence status or income.
> - Child: age band only (0–1, 1–3, 3–6, 6–10, 10+). Home: district only, never the street.
> - Quotes word for word, but replace names with [name] and place names with their type ([Kita], [Kinderarzt], [course]).
> - No audio, no photos, no video. Type up the notes the same day, then throw the paper away.
> - No contact list. If someone wants to try the app later, give them the app URL **[on the day]**. Do not take their number or email.
> - Adults only. Stop at once if someone hesitates.

## 1. Riskiest assumptions to test

Ordered by risk: A1 first.

| # | We believe | Confirmed if… | Refuted if… |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A1 An AI may call for me** | Parents let an AI assistant phone a place for them if it says it is an AI, they approve the German text first, and it shares only the child's age. | After the demo they name a real past situation where they would have used it, and what they need to see first. | They do not want an AI to call for them, expect the place to hang up, or do not want to share even the child's age. |
| **A2 The call is where they get stuck** | Finding a place is not the end. Parents stop at the phone call (German, work hours), and it hurts most with one type of place. We demo a **course**; maybe a **Kita** or the **Kinderarzt** is worse. | Stories of calls put off, avoided or handed to a partner, friend or colleague. One place type comes up again and again. | They call without trouble, or email and online booking already solve it. |
| **A3 Own language** | Parents want the app and the answer in their own language, not only English. Our 6 UI languages (en, de, ru, uk, ar, tr) cover the parents we meet. | They name a language they want the answer in, and it is one of the six. | English is enough for everyone, or they need a language we do not have. |
| **A4 Trust in a public listing** | Parents would trust a curated public list with a "checked by phone on …" date. Today they rely on chat groups, friends and Google Maps, and hit outdated information. | They tell a story of outdated or wrong information, and a date or a source builds trust for them. | They trust only friends' tips and would not use a public list, whatever it shows. |

## 3. Interview script (10 minutes)

**Rules:** ask about the past, not the future. Let silence work. Do not explain the product before the demo. Do not defend it after the demo.

### 3.1 Intro and consent (0:00–1:00)
*"Hi, I'm Abdul. We are a team at this event and we build a website for international parents in Dresden. I'm not selling anything, and there are no right answers. I want to hear how things really went for you."*
Then read the consent line (section 4) in their language. Go on only after a clear yes.

### 3.2 Warm-up (1:00–2:00)
- *"How long have you lived in Dresden?"*
- *"Which languages do you speak at home? How comfortable are you with German on the phone?"*
- *"How old is your child? A rough age is fine."* (write the band)
- *"Which part of Dresden do you live in?"* (district only)

### 3.3 Past behaviour (2:00–6:30): the core
Start with: *"Tell me about the last time you had to phone a Kita, a doctor's practice or a course for your child."*

Follow-ups (pick, do not read them all):
- *"What was it about?"* For a doctor: *"No need to tell me why, just how the call went."*
- *"Who made the call in the end? In which language?"* (A2, A3)
- *"What was the hardest part?"* (A2)
- *"Was there a call you kept putting off? What did you do instead?"* (A2)
- *"How long did it take until you had an answer?"* (A2)
- *"Where did you find that place in the first place?"* (A4)
- *"How did you know the information was still true? Tell me about a time it was wrong."* (A4)
- *"Which places are hardest for you to deal with: Kita, doctor, course, an office?"* (A2)

If they never had to call: *"How did you get the answer instead?"* That is data too.
Good prompts: *"Why?"* · *"What happened next?"* · *"Can you give me an example?"*

### 3.4 Demo moment (6:30–7:30, 30 seconds of showing)
Show, do not sell. Use what works on the day:
1. The Lovable app: provider page "Olgas Musikstudio" (demo data) → **Ask for me** → approval card (German line, their language below) → result card.
2. If the app is not ready: the finished example `/r/00000000-0000-0000-0000-000000000002` (pharmacy request in Arabic) or the backup video.
3. No screen: say the 30-second pitch from [[Pitch Kit v2 (merged)]] §1.

Say only: *"This is an early prototype. You find a place, tap 'Ask for me', check what the assistant will say in German, and approve. It says it is an AI, makes the call, and you get the answer in your language."*
Then stay quiet for 5 seconds. Write down the first reaction word for word.
Do not say that it already calls real places (we have role-plays only), or that it is "fully legal".

### 3.5 Reaction (7:30–9:00)
- *"What do you think is happening here?"*
- *"Where would you have used this in the last few months, if at all?"* (A1, A2)
- *"What would you need to see before it calls for you?"* (A1)
- *"What would stop you from using it?"* (A1)
- *"In which language would you want the answer?"* (A3)
- *"How would you decide whether to trust the information on this page?"* (A4)

### 3.6 Closing (9:00–10:00)
- *"What should I have asked you, but didn't?"*
- *"Thank you. My notes are anonymous. I won't use anything that could identify you."*
- If they want to try it: the app URL **[on the day]**. No contact details.

### Say this, not that

| Don't (leading or about the future) | Do (neutral, about the past) |
|---|---|
| "Is calling in German hard for you?" | "Tell me about the last call you made in German." |
| "Would you use an app that calls for you?" | "Where would you have used this in the last few months, if at all?" |
| "Don't you think this saves time?" | "What do you think is happening here?" |
| "You'd trust an AI that says it's an AI, right?" | "What would you need to see before it calls for you?" |
| "Do you prefer Russian?" | "Which languages do you speak at home?" |

## 6. From 3–5 interviews to 3 bullets

1. **Log (Sun by 11:45).** Create one run note, `Runs/RUN-0xx Parent interviews.md`, from `Templates/Run.md`, and paste in the filled tables. Only anonymous notes go into the repo. [[Pitch Kit v2 (merged)]] allows only numbers that are in a vault note, so this run note must exist before we say "3 of 5" on stage.
2. **Count** in a grid:

   | Assumption | P1 | P2 | P3 | P4 | P5 | Result |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
   | A1 An AI may call for me | | | | | | _ of N yes |
   | A2 The call is where they get stuck (+ top place type) | | | | | | _ of N yes · top: _ |
   | A3 Own language (+ languages named) | | | | | | _ of N yes |
   | A4 Trust in a public listing | | | | | | _ of N yes |

3. **Weigh.** A story from the past counts more than "I would use it". An assumption is **supported** when most parents (e.g. 3 of 5) confirm it with a story or a concrete condition, **refuted** when most say no, and **open** otherwise. With 3–5 people this is a first signal, not proof. Say so.
4. **Write 3 bullets.** Pick the 3 strongest findings. Each bullet has the form *what we heard → how many → what we do*. At least one should be a surprise or a refuted assumption.

   Shapes (replace the [ ]):
   - "[3] of [5] parents had put off a call to a [Kita], or had someone else make it; one said: '[quote]'. → [Kita waiting list] becomes the next *Ask for me* example, after courses."
   - "[4] of [5] would let an AI call if they see the German text first; [2] also wanted [X]. → Keep the approval card; add [X]."
   - "[Most] wanted the answer in [Russian / Arabic], not English, and [nobody] trusted a list without a date. → Keep the 6 UI languages and the 'checked by phone' date."

**Where the bullets go**
- **Demo (Sun 13:00):** one sentence in the "Ask" beat (2:50–3:00) or on slide 5 "What works, what is next": *"This weekend we talked to [N] international parents here. [Bullet 1, short]."* Say N honestly and call it a first signal.
- **Jury question "Did you talk to users?":** N, the 3 bullets, and the next step (8–10 more interviews).
- **[[Monday-Morning Plan]]:** add the 3 bullets under "Next 7 days", item 6 (Interviews). Supported → keep building. Refuted → new entry in "Risks & blockers". Open → first questions for the 8–10 parent interviews planned there.
